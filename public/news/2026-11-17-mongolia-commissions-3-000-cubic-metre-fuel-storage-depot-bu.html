Mongolia commissions 3,000 cubic metre fuel storage depot built by Mort Gobi Oil

A new 3,000 cubic metre petroleum products depot built by Mort Gobi Oil LLC entered service in Mongolia on 13 November 2026, part of a state-backed push to expand fuel reserves.

Supply17 Nov 2026Source: Ministry of Industry and Mineral Resources of Mongolia

A newly built storage depot for petroleum products with a total capacity of 3,000 cubic metres was put into operation in Mongolia on 13 November 2026, according to the Ministry of Industry and Mineral Resources. The facility was built by Mort Gobi Oil LLC and consists of tanks of 1,000 cubic metres each.

The ministry said it has been supporting companies in building and expanding warehouses and tanks by giving them access to concessional loans, with the aim of raising the country's capacity to hold fuel reserves. State Secretary of the ministry B. Dashpurev attended the opening ceremony.

The project forms part of the Government of Mongolia's 2024–2028 action programme, which sets a target of gradually increasing storage capacity for petroleum products so that the country can hold reserves equal to 3–6 months of consumption. Under this programme, new construction and expansion of warehouses and tanks with a combined capacity of 300 thousand cubic metres is being carried out.

To deliver the target, the ministry has worked with commercial banks to arrange concessional loan financing. As a result, storage construction and expansion work is under way at 22 locations operated by 18 companies, the ministry said.

According to the ministry, the additional storage capacity will allow larger volumes of imported fuel to be stockpiled, help the country cope with temporary supply disruptions and improve the stability of fuel supply to households and businesses.

  • New depot capacity: 3,000 cubic metres (tanks of 1,000 cubic metres each), commissioned 13 November 2026
  • National storage programme: 300 thousand cubic metres of new and expanded capacity
  • Reserve target: 3–6 months of consumption under the 2024–2028 action programme
  • Projects under way: 22 locations, 18 companies
